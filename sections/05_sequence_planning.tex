\section{Sequence Planning} \label{sec:sequence_planning}

In this section, we introduce the remaining modules $\initpose$ and $\simul$ that are used to generate candidate poses and simulate the stability of the scene, respectively.
Then, we present the Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm that uses these modules to explore the space of possible stacking sequences and find an optimal sequence of object placements.

\subsection{Initial Pose Sampling}
The sampler proposes an object $i$, orientation $R$, and planar location $\planar$ on a feasible grid $\mathcal{G}_{i,R}(\state_k)$ according to
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \initpose(i,R,\planar\mid\state_k)
        &= \pi_{xy}(\planar\mid i,R,\state_k)
           \pi_\text{unif}(i,R\mid\state_k),\\
        \pi_{xy}(\planar\mid i,R,\state_k)
        &\propto
        \exp\!\left(\score(\planar\mid i,R,\state_k)/\tau\right).
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where $\pi_\text{unif}$ is uniform over the remaining objects
$i\in\inventory_k$ and finite rotation set $\mathcal{R}$, and $\tau>0$ controls the preference for high scores. Scene geometry determines the initial height $z_{i,R}$, yielding
$\hat q_{k+1}=(R,(\planar,z_{i,R}(\planar;\state_k)))$ for refinement by $\poseopt$. Omitting the state dependence, the proposal score is
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \score(\planar\mid i_{k+1},R) ={}& w_\text{s}\score_\text{s}(\planar\mid i_{k+1},R)+w_\text{f}\score_\text{f}(\planar\mid i_{k+1},R) \\
    &+w_\text{b}\score_\text{b}(\planar\mid i_{k+1},R)-w_\text{h}\score_\text{h}(\planar\mid i_{k+1},R),
  \end{split}
%   \label{eq:initial_pose_score}
\end{equation*}
with positive weights. The support score $\score_\text{s}$ favors larger support areas, $\score_\text{f}$ rewards filling empty floor area, $\score_\text{b}$ favors the target boundary, and $\score_\text{h}$ penalizes height. The floor and boundary terms are inspired by the dense first-course packing and boundary-first behavior observed by Menezes et al.~\cite{menezes2021rocks}; the height penalty follows the low-placement preference of their geometric baseline.
We use these preferences to sample initial poses for continuous refinement.
Figure~\ref{fig:score_map} illustrates these score maps; their computation is deferred to the appendix.

\subsection{Simulation Module}

After $\poseopt$ refines the candidate pose $\hat{q}_{k+1}$, the simulation
module $\simul$ evaluates the deterministic transition
$\state_{k+1}=\transition(\state_k,\action_k)$. It first simulates the new
object with the existing scene fixed to refine the placement, and then
simulates the entire scene to verify global stability.

Both stages use early termination. The simulation stops when all placed
objects have sufficiently small kinetic energy or when accumulated motion
energy indicates instability. For object $\object_i$, we define
\begin{align*}
    E_{\text{kin},i}(t) = \frac{1}{2m_i}\mathcal{V}_i^\top M_i \mathcal{V}_i, \quad 
    E_{\text{acc},i}(t) = \sum_{s=0}^{t} \sqrt{E_{\text{kin},i}(s)} \Delta t
\end{align*}
where $\mathcal{V}_i$ is the spatial velocity, $M_i$ and $m_i$ are the mass
matrix and mass, respectively, and $\Delta t$ is the simulation time step.
The early-termination condition is
\begin{equation}
    \max_{i \in \placed_{k+1}} E_{\text{kin},i}(t) < \epsilon_\text{kin}
    \quad\text{or}\quad
    \max_{i \in \placed_{k+1}} E_{\text{acc},i}(t) > \epsilon_\text{acc}
\end{equation}
with the thresholds $\epsilon_\text{kin}, \epsilon_\text{acc} > 0$.
We implement $\simul$ in CUDA using the GPU-accelerated subADMM algorithm
\cite{ji2025gpu}.

\subsection{Reward Design}

The reward balances target coverage, object containment, surface uniformity, and physical stability:
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        r(\state_k, \action_k) 
        ={}& w_\target\frac{\vol\left(\overline{\scene}_{k+1}\right) - \vol\left(\overline{\scene}_k\right)}{\vol(\target)} + w_\object\frac{\vol\left(O_{i_{k+1}}\cap\target\right)}{\vol\left(O_{i_{k+1}}\right)} \\
        &- w_H\left(\max_{\planar}H_{\scene_{k+1}}(\planar) - \mathbb{E}_{\planar}[H_{\scene_{k+1}}(\planar)]\right) \\
        & - w_D\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{V}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)}\left[\Delta q_{k+1}(\mathcal{V})\right]- w_S f_\text{eq}
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:reward_function}
\end{equation*}
where all weights are positive,
$\overline{\scene}_k:=\target\cap\bigcup_{i\in\placed_k}\object_i(q_i)$ is the occupied target region, and $H_{\scene_{k+1}}(\planar)$ is the upper surface height at $\planar=(x,y)$. 
The first two terms reward newly filled target volume and the fraction of the candidate contained in the target, respectively. 
The remaining terms penalize surface height variation, the candidate's expected pose deviation $\Delta q_{k+1}(\mathcal V)$ under Gaussian perturbations $\mathcal V\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$, and the equilibrium residual $f_\text{eq}$ from $\poseopt$.

\subsection{Monte Carlo Tree Search}
We combine $\initpose$, $\poseopt$, and $\simul$ in a batched, receding-horizon MCTS planner over the MDP defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:overview}. To amortize expensive pose optimization and simulation, we evaluate candidate placements in parallel, following the batched planning approach of~\cite{huang2022pmbs}.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:mcts} summarizes the finite-horizon search and execution procedure, starting from a tree rooted at the current state $\state_k$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Batched receding-horizon MCTS for sequence planning}
    \label{alg:mcts}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require State $\state_k$, previous search tree $\tree$, batch size $B$
        \Ensure Next state $\state_{k+1}$ and updated tree $\tree$
        \State Initialize or update $\tree$ at $\state_k$ and evaluate root candidates
        \While{the search budget remains}
            \State $\batch\gets\texttt{SELECTBATCH}(\tree,B)$
            \State $\mathcal{E}\gets\texttt{EVALUATEBATCH}(\batch,\initpose,\poseopt,\simul)$
            \State $\tree\gets\texttt{BACKUP}(\tree,\batch,\mathcal{E})$
        \EndWhile
        \State $\action^*\gets\argmax_{\action\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{val}}(\state_k)} Q(\node_{\action})$
        \State Execute $\action^*$ and obtain $\state_{k+1}$
        \State Promote its child to the root and retain compatible descendants
        \State \Return $\state_{k+1},\tree$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent \textbf{Batched Selection.}
We select a batch of expansion paths using PUCT~\cite{silver2017mastering}
with geometry- and equilibrium-informed action priors. For a child $\node$
reached from $\node_p$ by action $a$, selection maximizes
\begin{equation}
    \puct(\node) =
    \frac{\visit(\node)Q(\node)}{\visit(\node)+\tmpvisit(\node)}
    + cP(\node_p,a)
    \frac{\sqrt{\visit(\node_p)+\tmpvisit(\node_p)}}
         {1+\visit(\node)+\tmpvisit(\node)},
    \label{eq:puct}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is the mean return, $\visit$ and $\tmpvisit$ are completed and
temporary virtual visit counts, $P$ is the action prior, and $c>0$ controls
exploration. Progressive widening allows more children as completed visits
increase, while virtual visits diversify selection within each batch.
All paths are selected before physical evaluation begins.

\noindent \textbf{Parallel Expansion and Evaluation.}
Each selected expansion applies $\initpose$, $\poseopt$, and $\simul$ to
generate, refine, and physically evaluate a candidate placement.
These evaluations run in parallel and provide settled successor states,
validity outcomes, and rewards for tree expansion and backup. Root candidates
are evaluated through the same pipeline.

\noindent \textbf{Leaf Value Estimation and Backup.}
To avoid further optimization and simulation costs, we replace additional
rollouts with a heuristic estimate of the remaining return. For a nonterminal
leaf $\state_l$ at depth $\depth_l$ from the current root, we use
\begin{equation*}
    \hat V(\state_l)=\bar r\sum_{j=0}^{h_l-1}\gamma^j,
    \qquad
    h_l=\min\!\left\{\depth_M-\depth_l,|\inventory(\state_l)|\right\},
\end{equation*}
where $\depth_M$ is the maximum search depth, $\inventory(\state_l)$ is the
remaining inventory, and $\bar r$ is the mean reward estimated from evaluated
placements. Terminal and failed leaves receive no bootstrap value.
Discounted placement rewards and the leaf estimate are backed up along each
selected path to update mean returns $Q$ and visit counts $\visit$.
Temporary virtual visits are then removed.

\noindent \textbf{Action Selection and Tree Reuse.}
After the search budget is exhausted, we execute the physically validated
root action with the highest mean return. In Algorithm~\ref{alg:mcts},
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{val}}(\state_k)$ denotes these validated actions and
$\node_a$ the child reached by $a$. The selected child becomes the new root,
retaining descendants and search statistics compatible with the resulting
state. Planning then repeats with a finite lookahead horizon.
