\section{Sequence Planning} \label{sec:sequence_planning}

\subsection{Initial Pose Sampling}
The sampler $\initpose$ uses volume-biased object sampling with a finite orientation set per object. It prioritizes the lowest incomplete layer and seeds empty scenes near feasible target corners. For object $i$, orientation $R$, and feasible planar location $\planar$, the score is
\begin{equation*}
  \score(\planar\mid i,R)
  =w_\text{s}\score_\text{s}+w_\text{f}\score_\text{f}
   +w_\text{b}\score_\text{b}-w_\text{h}\score_\text{h}-w_\text{d}\score_\text{d},
\end{equation*}
with positive weights. The first four terms measure the fraction of bottom samples near predicted contact, unfilled layer area beneath the footprint, proximity to the feasible placement boundary, and normalized mean contact height, respectively. The distance penalty $\score_\text{d}=[d_\mathrm{prev}-\delta]_+/(L+[d_\mathrm{prev}-\delta]_+)$ uses the minimum gap $d_\mathrm{prev}$ between the convex XY footprints of the candidate and preceding object, gap tolerance $\delta\geq0$, and length scale $L$, the cube root of the initial inventory's mean object volume. Favoring nearby placements helps MCTS lookahead assess interactions between successive objects; distant proposals remain eligible. The first placement incurs no penalty. The floor and boundary preferences are inspired by Menezes et al.~\cite{menezes2021rocks}. Figure~\ref{fig:score_map} illustrates the proposal score and sampled initial pose.

We combine elite selection with sampling without replacement weighted by $\exp(\score/\tau)$, $\tau>0$. Proposals start above the scene with clearance and undergo $\poseopt$ refinement, including XY/yaw mutations at the root. To preserve locality, both optimized and settled XY positions must stay within $\eta\vol(\object_i)^{1/3}$ of the proposal's initial XY position, $\eta>0$. Refined poses' lowest points must not exceed the active-layer height plus a tolerance; projected coverage determines layer advancement. Blocked expansion may retry without this layer restriction within the remaining budget; other admission checks remain.

\subsection{Simulation Module}\label{sec:simulation}
The module $\simul$ evaluates every proposed transition using the same simulation procedure at all search depths.
It first settles the new object for a fixed duration while holding the existing objects static.
From the resulting poses, it resets velocities and simulates the entire structure's response to the added load.
Structural simulation requires a minimum duration and sustained low mass-normalized kinetic energy.
Excessive accumulated motion, invalid contacts, or failure to settle within the time limit rejects the placement.

For an accepted nominal simulation, the settled scene defines the successor $\state'=\transition(\state,\action)$.
Each disturbance trial starts from this scene with the new object's initial world-frame linear velocity
\begin{equation*}
 \xi_j=\bigl(v_h\cos\theta_j,\;v_h\sin\theta_j,\;-v_d\bigr)^\top,
\end{equation*}
where $\theta_j=2\pi j/n_d$ for $j=0,\ldots,n_d-1$, and $v_h,v_d>0$ are prescribed horizontal and downward speeds.
Initial angular velocity is zero, and every candidate uses the same $n_d$ disturbances.
To reduce computation, only the candidate and stones within two links in the nominal scene's contact graph of movable stones remain dynamic throughout each trial.
All other bodies retain fixed collision geometry.
These trials estimate local robustness without changing $\state'$.
The resulting successor, reward, and feasibility outcome are stored in the tree and used directly for subsequent search and action selection.
Simulation uses CUDA subADMM~\cite{ji2025gpu}.

\subsection{Reward Design}
For the newly placed object $i=i_{k+1}$, let $q_i'$ be its pose in the settled successor $s'$ returned by $\simul$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:simulation}). We define the target-volume and object-volume ratios
\begin{equation*}
 \rho_\target=\frac{\vol\bigl(\object_i(q_i')\cap\target\bigr)}{\vol(\target)},
 \quad
 \rho_\object=\frac{\vol\bigl(\object_i(q_i')\cap\target\bigr)}{\vol(\object_i)}.
\end{equation*}
The $\rho_\target$ term encourages target filling toward the objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:problem_definition}, while $\rho_\object$ discourages placements extending excessively outside the target. Intersection volumes are estimated on a voxel grid. With fixed normalization scales absorbed into positive weights, the standard excavator reward is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
 r(\state,\action)={}&w_\target\rho_\target+w_\object[\rho_\object-\tfrac12]_+
 -w_E e(\action)\\
 &+w_H\bigl(\phi_H(\state)-\phi_H(\state')\bigr)
 -w_D D,
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
where $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$, so the containment bonus is active above $50\%$. Coverage lost through motion of earlier objects is not subtracted. The height term rewards the decrease in surface-height variance $\phi_H(s)=\langle(H_s-\langle H_s\rangle_\Omega)^2\rangle_\Omega$ between parent and settled successor. Here $H_s$ is height over the target footprint $\Omega$, zero at empty cells, and $\langle\cdot\rangle_\Omega$ denotes the area-weighted mean.

The equilibrium score uses residual forces $\mathbf f_l$ and torques $\boldsymbol\tau_l$ returned by pose optimization:
\begin{equation*}
 e(\action)=\min\!\left\{1,\max_l\left(
 \frac{\|\mathbf f_l\|}{m_lg}
 +\frac{\|\boldsymbol\tau_l\|}{m_lg\vol(\object_l)^{1/3}}\right)\right\}.
\end{equation*}
Here $l$ indexes objects in the resulting structure and $g$ is gravitational acceleration.
Let $q_l(t;\xi_j)$ denote object $l$'s pose under the predefined velocity disturbance $\xi_j$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:simulation}), starting from its nominal successor pose $q_l'$.
Disturbance sensitivity is
\begin{equation*}
 D=\min\!\left\{D_{\max},\max_{j,l,t}d_{\SE(3)}\bigl(q_l(t;\xi_j),q_l'\bigr)\right\},
\end{equation*}
where $d_{\SE(3)}$ sums Euclidean translation distance and angular distance weighted by $\lambda_\theta>0$.
The maximum spans predefined disturbances, objects $l$ in this local dynamic neighborhood, and simulation time; $D_{\max}$ caps the penalty. Physical rejection criteria apply independently of the reward.

\subsection{Monte Carlo Tree Search}
We use batched, receding-horizon MCTS with parallel evaluation~\cite{huang2022pmbs} (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

\noindent \textbf{Batched Selection.}
For an optimized action $a=(i,q)$, let $\rho_\object^{\mathrm{pre}}$ denote the object-volume ratio evaluated at $q$, before simulation. Using the reward weights, the logit $\ell(a)=w_\object\rho_\object^{\mathrm{pre}}-w_E e(a)$ defines $P(\node_p,a)$ through a softmax over selectable children. Writing $N_n=N(n)$ and similarly for $Q_n$ and $\hat N_n$, an evaluated child $n$ of $n_p$ is selected using PUCT~\cite{silver2017mastering}:
\begin{equation}
    \puct(n)=\frac{N_nQ_n}{N_n+\hat N_n}
    +cP(n_p,a)\frac{\sqrt{N_{n_p}+\hat N_{n_p}}}
         {1+N_n+\hat N_n},
    \label{eq:puct}
\end{equation}
% tree_policy.selection_score requires positive effective parent visits;
% pending actions are handled before PUCT, so no max(..., 1) is used here.
where $c>0$ controls exploration, $\tmpvisit$ counts temporary reservations, and the first term is zero when its denominator is zero.
At non-root nodes, progressive widening~\cite{couetoux2011continuousuct} increases the number of candidate actions with completed visits.
Temporary reservations prevent duplicate evaluations within a batch without increasing the widening allowance.

\noindent \textbf{Parallel Expansion and Evaluation.}
After batch selection, fresh proposals pass through $\initpose\to\poseopt\to\simul$. Independent candidates are processed in parallel; selection never descends through an action without an evaluated successor. Failed candidates are excluded from further selection.

\noindent \textbf{Leaf Value Estimation and Backpropagation.}
We replace rollouts with a discounted per-step reward heuristic, capped by depth and remaining inventory. Terminal, failed, and depth-limit leaves receive no bootstrap; exhausted shallower nonterminal leaves are not repeatedly backed up. Failures incur a discounted fixed per-step penalty over the remaining horizon, capped by inventory. Backpropagation updates $Q$ and $N$ and clears temporary reservations.

\noindent \textbf{Action Selection and Tree Reuse.}
Evaluated feasible root actions are ranked by $(1-\alpha)Q(\node_a)+\alpha G_{\mathrm{obs}}(\node_a)$, $\alpha\in[0,1]$. Here $G_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the best discounted return on each candidate's deepest evaluated feasible paths within the horizon, without bootstrap; root-only paths contribute their immediate reward.

After execution, we retain successor subtrees with matching states and planning contexts. Other subtrees and branches with relaxed layer admission are discarded, resetting statistics before reuse.
