\section{Sequence Planning} \label{sec:sequence_planning}

\subsection{Initial Pose Sampling}
The sampler $\initpose$ allocates fresh proposals to remaining objects in proportion to their volumes and considers a finite set of orientation hypotheses for each object. It prioritizes connected growth on the lowest incomplete layer, seeding an empty scene near feasible target corners. For an object $i$, orientation $R$, and feasible planar location $\planar$, the geometric score is
\begin{equation*}
  \score(\planar\mid i,R)
  =w_\text{s}\score_\text{s}+w_\text{f}\score_\text{f}
   +w_\text{b}\score_\text{b}-w_\text{h}\score_\text{h},
\end{equation*}
with positive weights. The terms measure the fraction of bottom samples near predicted contact, unfilled layer area beneath the footprint, proximity to the feasible placement boundary, and normalized mean contact height, respectively. The floor and boundary preferences are inspired by Menezes et al.~\cite{menezes2021rocks}. Figure~\ref{fig:score_map} illustrates the resulting proposal score and sampled initial pose.

Within eligible pools, the standard sampler combines elite selection with draws without replacement weighted by $\exp(\score/\tau)$, $\tau>0$. Each selected hypothesis is initialized above overlapping scene geometry with a clearance, then refined by $\poseopt$ and filtered for feasibility. At the search root, population refinement additionally mixes fresh proposals with planar-position and yaw mutations of promising candidates, followed by renewed pose optimization.

\subsection{Simulation Module}\label{sec:simulation}
The module $\simul$ first settles the new object for a fixed duration with the existing scene static, then simulates the structure. Exploration uses short evaluations that may freeze distant objects while preserving the candidate's support chain. Root placements and final continuation paths receive longer validation without distance-based freezing; explicitly fixed bodies remain static.

Structural settling requires a minimum duration and sustained low mass-normalized kinetic energy. Excessive accumulated motion, invalid contacts, or timeout without settling rejects the placement. We store the settled scene as the successor $\state'=\transition(\state,\action)$. Separate trials start from this scene and apply Gaussian velocity disturbances to the new object, measuring motion of all dynamic bodies. These trials supply robustness scores but do not alter $\state'$. Simulation uses CUDA subADMM~\cite{ji2025gpu}.

\subsection{Reward Design}
Let $q_i'$ be object $i$'s pose in the settled successor $s'$ returned by $\simul$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:simulation}). We define the target-volume and object-volume ratios
\begin{equation*}
 \rho_\target=\frac{\vol\bigl(\object_i(q_i')\cap\target\bigr)}{\vol(\target)},
 \quad
 \rho_\object=\frac{\vol\bigl(\object_i(q_i')\cap\target\bigr)}{\vol(\object_i)}.
\end{equation*}
The former measures the new object's contribution to filling the target; the latter measures its containment. Intersection volumes are estimated on a voxel grid. With fixed normalization scales absorbed into positive weights, the standard excavator reward is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
 r(\state,\action)={}&w_\target\rho_\target+w_\object[\rho_\object-\tfrac12]_+
 -w_E e(\action)\\
 &-w_H\bigl(\max_{\Omega}H_{\state'}-\langle H_{\state'}\rangle_{\Omega}\bigr)
 -w_D\widetilde D,
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
where $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$, so the containment bonus is active above $50\%$. Coverage lost through motion of earlier objects is not subtracted. $H_{\state'}$ is surface height over the target footprint $\Omega$, with zero at empty cells; $\langle\cdot\rangle_\Omega$ is its spatial mean.

The equilibrium score uses residual forces $\mathbf f_b$ and torques $\boldsymbol\tau_b$ returned by pose optimization:
\begin{equation*}
 e(\action)=\min\!\left\{1,\max_b\left(
 \frac{\|\mathbf f_b\|}{m_bg}
 +\frac{\|\boldsymbol\tau_b\|}{m_bg\vol(\object_b)^{1/3}}\right)\right\}.
\end{equation*}
Here $b$ indexes participating bodies and $g$ is gravitational acceleration. For disturbance trial $j$, dynamic body $b$, and time $t$, define
\begin{equation*}
 D=\max_{j,b,t}\left[
 \|p_{j,b}(t)-p_b^0\|
 +\lambda_\theta d_{\SO(3)}(R_{j,b}(t),R_b^0)\right],
\end{equation*}
where $(p_b^0,R_b^0)$ is body $b$'s pose in the same successor $s'$, $d_{\SO(3)}$ is angular distance, and $\lambda_\theta$ scales rotation. The capped excursion $\widetilde D$ measures the worst transient motion across trials and dynamic bodies. Physical rejection criteria apply independently of the reward.

\subsection{Monte Carlo Tree Search}
We integrate these modules in batched, receding-horizon MCTS (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), following the parallel evaluation approach of~\cite{huang2022pmbs}.

\noindent \textbf{Batched Selection.}
For an optimized action $a=(i,q)$, let $\rho_\object^{\mathrm{pre}}$ denote the object-volume ratio evaluated at $q$, before simulation. The logit $\ell(a)=2(\rho_\object^{\mathrm{pre}}-e(a))$ defines $P(\node_p,a)$ through a softmax over selectable children; root refinement adds the normalized proposal score to $\ell$ for ranking. Writing $N_n=N(n)$ and similarly for $Q_n$ and $\hat N_n$, an evaluated child $n$ of $n_p$ is selected using PUCT~\cite{silver2017mastering}:
\begin{equation}
    \puct(n)=\frac{N_nQ_n}{N_n+\hat N_n}
    +cP(n_p,a)\frac{\sqrt{\max\{N_{n_p}+\hat N_{n_p},1\}}}
         {1+N_n+\hat N_n},
    \label{eq:puct}
\end{equation}
where $c>0$ controls exploration, $\tmpvisit$ counts temporary reservations, and the first term is zero when its denominator is zero. Non-root progressive widening permits
$K(\node)=\min\{16,\lceil3\max(\visit(\node),1)^{1/2}\rceil\}$
children based on completed visits; root capacity is set separately. Selection reserves pending actions or new proposal slots, prioritizing available pending actions before PUCT descent through evaluated children. Reservations prevent duplicate work and do not increase the widening allowance.

\noindent \textbf{Parallel Expansion and Evaluation.}
After batch selection, fresh proposals pass through $\initpose\to\poseopt\to\simul$, whereas cached optimized actions enter at simulation. Independent candidates are processed in parallel; selection never descends through an action without an evaluated successor. Failed candidates are excluded from further selection.

\noindent \textbf{Leaf Value Estimation and Backpropagation.}
We replace additional rollouts with a discounted fixed per-step reward heuristic over a capped horizon, limited by search depth and remaining inventory. Terminal and failed leaves receive no bootstrap. Discounted returns update $Q$ and $N$ along selected paths, with a failure reward propagated to ancestors of rejected candidates. Temporary reservations are then cleared.

\noindent \textbf{Action Selection and Tree Reuse.}
Among root actions with continuations validated by longer simulations, we maximize
$J(a)=(1-\beta)Q(\node_a)+\beta G_{\mathrm{val}}(a)$,
where $\beta\in[0,1]$ and $G_{\mathrm{val}}$ is the validated path return, including the current placement. If bounded repair finds no such path, we fall back to a physically validated root action ranked by mean return. After execution, we update the root from observation, revalidate reused candidates, discard inconsistent descendants, and reset search statistics.
