\section{Sequence Planning via Parallel MCTS}\label{sec:sequence_planning}

Building on Sec.~\ref{sec:overview}, we combine batched MCTS~\cite{huang2022pmbs} with parallel pose optimization, sampling, simulation, and domain-specific rewards (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).
In receding-horizon planning, we search a limited horizon, execute one selected placement, and replan from the observed scene.

\subsection{Batched Search Procedure}\label{sec:mcts}

\noindent \textbf{Batched Selection.}
Colored paths in the ``Batched selection'' panel (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}) terminate at expansion requests $b_j$.
Over selectable child actions of $\node_p$, we define
\begin{equation*}
 P(\node_p,a)=\operatorname{softmax}_{a}\!\left(w_\object\rho_\object^{\mathrm{pre}}(a)-w_E e(a)\right),
\end{equation*}
where $\rho_\object^{\mathrm{pre}}(a)$ is the pre-simulation object-volume fraction inside the target; $e(a)$ and the weights follow Sec.~\ref{sec:reward}.
Writing $N_{\node}=N(\node)$ and similarly for $Q,\hat N$, we select evaluated children using PUCT~\cite{silver2017mastering}:
\begin{equation}
    \puct(\node)=\frac{N_{\node}Q_{\node}}{N_{\node}+\hat N_{\node}}
    +cP(\node_p,a)\frac{\sqrt{N_{\node_p}+\hat N_{\node_p}}}
         {1+N_{\node}+\hat N_{\node}},
    \label{eq:puct}
\end{equation}
where $c>0$ controls exploration, $\tmpvisit$ counts temporary reservations, and the first term is zero when its denominator is zero.
We use progressive widening~\cite{couetoux2011continuousuct} during tree search to expand the set of candidate placements as visits accumulate.
Selection follows evaluated, feasible children using PUCT until a node is chosen for expansion.
Reservations prevent duplicate batch evaluations without increasing the widening allowance.

\noindent \textbf{Parallel Expansion and Evaluation.}
Each colored row in Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} evaluates one request through $\initpose\to\poseopt\to\simul$; rows run in parallel using the pose optimizer of Sec.~\ref{sec:pose_optimization}.

\noindent \textbf{Leaf Value Estimation and Backpropagation.}
For nonterminal, feasible leaves, we replace rollouts with $\hat V(\node)=\hat r\sum_{j=0}^{\depth_{\node}-1}\gamma^j$, where $\depth_{\node}$ counts future placements in the estimate, capped by remaining search depth and inventory.
The fixed hyperparameter $\hat r$ sets a heuristic per-step reward for unexplored continuations, influencing exploration through backpropagated returns alongside PUCT.
Failed or terminal nodes have $\hat V=0$.
Following the arrows in the ``Backpropagation'' panel (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), we propagate $r_{\node}+\gamma\hat V(\node)$, with incoming reward $r_{\node}$, toward the root, adding discounted ancestor rewards, updating $Q,N$, and clearing reservations.

\noindent \textbf{Action Selection and Tree Reuse.}
As shown at the bottom right of Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}, we select the feasible root action maximizing
\begin{equation*}
  J(a)=(1-\beta)Q(\node_a)+\beta G(a),\qquad \beta\in[0,1].
\end{equation*}
Here $\node_a$ is the root child reached by $a$, and $G(a)$ is the maximum discounted observed return over the deepest evaluated feasible paths below $a$, including its reward but excluding bootstrap values; root-only paths are eligible.
This term offsets exploration failures that depress $Q$ despite promising paths. If no root action is feasible, no placement is returned.

After execution, the observed successor becomes the root.
We retain descendants and statistics only if the observed state and planning context match the subtree, discarding it otherwise.

\subsection{Initial Pose Sampling}\label{sec:initial_pose_sampling}
The sampler $\initpose$ gives larger objects more proposal attempts, uses finite orientation sets, and seeds empty scenes near feasible target corners.
For object $i$, orientation $R$, and feasible planar location $\planar$, the score (Fig.~\ref{fig:score_map}) is
\begin{equation*}
  \score(\planar\mid i,R)
  =w_\text{s}\score_\text{s}+w_\text{f}\score_\text{f}
   +w_\text{b}\score_\text{b}-w_\text{h}\score_\text{h}-w_\text{d}\score_\text{d},
\end{equation*}
with positive weights. The terms measure predicted bottom support fraction, unfilled layer area beneath the footprint, feasible-boundary proximity, normalized mean contact height, and a bounded footprint-gap penalty to the preceding object (zero for the first placement).
Following Menezes et al.~\cite{menezes2021rocks}, floor and boundary preferences encourage filling empty regions and building inward from the boundary.

We combine elite selection with sampling without replacement weighted by $\exp(\score/\tau)$, $\tau>0$.
We initialize height so the candidate's lowest point lies above the base plane and the bounding-box tops of objects with overlapping padded XY bounds, with added clearance (Fig.~\ref{fig:score_map}(c)).
Geometric filters constrain planar displacement and placement height after refinement and settling.

\subsection{Simulation Module}\label{sec:simulation}
At every search depth, $\simul$ first settles the candidate for a fixed time duration with existing objects fixed, then resets velocities and simulates the entire structure.
Settling requires a minimum duration and sustained low mass-normalized kinetic energy; excessive accumulated motion, invalid contacts, or timeout reject the placement.

The settled scene defines $\state'=\transition(\state,\action)$.
From rest in $\state'$, independent disturbance trials apply a force of magnitude $\alpha m_{i_{k+1}}g$ at the candidate's center of mass for $\Delta t_f$, in $n_d$ evenly spaced horizontal directions ($\alpha>0$, gravitational acceleration $g$).
With all objects dynamic, each trial must settle again without exceeding prescribed displacement limits relative to $\state'$; trials do not alter $\state'$.
GPU-accelerated subADMM~\cite{ji2025gpu} evaluates independent candidates in parallel.

\subsection{Reward Design}\label{sec:reward}
For the new object $i=i_{k+1}$ at its settled successor pose $q_i'$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:simulation}), define
\begin{equation*}
 \rho_\target=\frac{\vol\bigl(\object_i(q_i')\cap\target\bigr)}{\vol(\target)},
 \quad
 \rho_\object=\frac{\vol\bigl(\object_i(q_i')\cap\target\bigr)}{\vol(\object_i)}.
\end{equation*}
These voxel-estimated volume ratios reward target filling and containment. For feasible transitions, with fixed normalization scales absorbed into positive weights,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
 r(\state,\action)={}&w_\target\rho_\target+w_\object[\rho_\object-\tfrac12]_+
 -w_E e(\action)\\
 &+w_H\bigl(\phi_H(\state)-\phi_H(\state')\bigr)
 -w_D D,
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
where $\phi_H(s)=\operatorname{Var}_\Omega(H_s)$ is the area-weighted variance of scene height over the target footprint $\Omega$, with empty cells assigned zero height.
Coverage lost through earlier objects' motion is not subtracted. Failed transitions receive $r=-1$.

For pose-optimization residual forces $\mathbf f_l$ and torques $\boldsymbol\tau_l$ across all placed objects,
\begin{equation*}
 e(\action)=\max_l\left(
 \frac{\|\mathbf f_l\|}{m_lg}
 +\frac{\|\boldsymbol\tau_l\|}{m_lg\vol(\object_l)^{1/3}}\right).
\end{equation*}
Candidates with $e(\action)\geq\epsilon_\mathrm{eq}$ are rejected before simulation.
Disturbance sensitivity measures motion from settled poses $q_l'$ during force trials:
\begin{equation*}
 D=\min\!\left\{D_{\max},\max_{j,l,t}d_{\SE(3)}\bigl(q_l^{(j)}(t),q_l'\bigr)\right\},
\end{equation*}
Here $q_l^{(j)}(t)$ is object $l$'s pose in trial $j$ at time $t$. The maximum spans all trials, objects, and simulation times including recovery.
The distance $d_{\SE(3)}$ sums translation distance and angular distance weighted by $\lambda_\theta>0$.
