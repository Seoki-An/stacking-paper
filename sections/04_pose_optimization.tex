\section{Pose Optimization} \label{sec:pose_optimization}

The module $\poseopt$ refines $\hat q_{k+1}$ from $\initpose$ toward a stable,
physically feasible placement $q_{k+1}$.

\subsection{Single Object Pose Optimization Problem}

Given $\state_k$, we initialize $q$ at $\hat q_{k+1}$ and solve the bilevel
problem
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
  \min_{q} &{\quad}& \left[f_\text{geo}(q) + \min_{\lambda} f_\text{phys}(q, \lambda)\right] \label{eq:pose_optimization} \\
  \text{s.t. } &{\quad}& g_{\text{cone},j}(q,\lambda_j) \leq 0, \quad j=1,\cdots,n_c \nonumber
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where $q \in \SE(3)$ is the candidate object's pose and $\lambda = (\lambda_1,\cdots,\lambda_{n_c}) \in \real^{3n_c}$ collects all contact forces in the resulting structure, including inter-object and ground contacts.
This formulation follows prior contact-aware optimization methods \cite{lee2025contactfactorgraph,chen2025bodex}.

% The geometric objective $f_\text{geo}(q)$ measures geometric quality, including target shape approximation and collision avoidance; its detailed formulation is provided in the appendix.
The geometric objective $f_\text{geo}$ is designed as follows:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    f_\text{geo}(q)
    ={} & w_\text{h} f_\text{h}(q) + w_\text{in}f_\text{in}(q \mid \target, \object_{i_{k+1}}) \\
        & + w_\text{pen}f_\text{pen}(q \mid \scene_k, \object_{i_{k+1}})
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
with positive weights. For $q=(R,t)$, $f_\text{h}(q)=t_z$ is the world-frame height of the candidate's pose origin.
Writing $\object=\object_{i_{k+1}}$, we penalize proximity to existing objects and the fixed ground, indexed by $i_0$, as
\begin{equation*}
  f_\text{pen}(q)=\sum_{j\in\placed_k\cup\{i_0\}}
  \bigl[\epsilon_\text{margin}-\sd(\object(q),\object_j(q_j))\bigr]_+^2,
\end{equation*}
where $\sd$ is signed distance (positive for separation), $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$, and $\epsilon_\text{margin}\geq0$ is the clearance margin.
With $\dsf_{\object(q)}(u)=\max_j\dsf_{\convex_{i_{k+1},j}(q)}(u)$, we define
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{aligned}
    m_\text{in}(q) & =\max_j\min_{u\in\mathbb{S}^2}\bigl[\dsf_{\target_j}(u)-\dsf_{\object(q)}(u)\bigr], \\
    f_\text{in}(q) & =\bigl[-m_\text{in}(q)\bigr]_+^2.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
This term penalizes violations of containment of the entire object within a single convex target component, a conservative condition for containment in $\target$.
It is differentiable almost everywhere in $q$; we compute pose derivatives at regular inner solutions by implicit differentiation.
Nonsmooth transitions have not caused practical difficulties in our implementation.

Let $\placed_{k+1}:=\placed_k\cup\{i_{k+1}\}$, excluding the ground index
$i_0$. For distinct $l,m\in\placed_{k+1}\cup\{i_0\}$, let
$I_{l,m}\subset\{1,\ldots,n_c\}$ index contacts between bodies $l$ and $m$.
Each object's contact set
$I_l:=\bigcup_{m\in(\placed_{k+1}\cup\{i_0\})\setminus\{l\}}I_{l,m}$ includes
ground contacts, but the physical objective excludes the ground's equilibrium
residual:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    f_\text{phys}(q,\lambda) \label{eq:force_objective}
    ={} & w_\text{eq}\sum_{l \in \placed_{k+1}} \|J_{c,I_l}^\top(q)  \lambda_{I_l} + \lambda_{\text{ext},l}\|^2            \\
        & + w_\text{cp}\sum_{j \in I_{i_{k+1}}}\sd_j(q)^2 \|\lambda_j\|^2 + w_\text{reg}\sum_{j = 1}^{n_c} \|\lambda_j\|^2
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
The three terms respectively penalize violations of static equilibrium, contact complementarity between the candidate and the current scene, and large contact forces through regularization.
Here, $\sd_j(q)$ is the signed distance between the two bodies associated with contact candidate $j$. $J_{c,I_l}$ is the contact Jacobian for object $\object_l$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{ext},l}$ is its external wrench, $\lambda_{I_l}$ is the vector of contact forces at the contact points in $I_l$, and $w_\mathrm{eq},w_\mathrm{cp},w_\mathrm{reg}>0$ are objective weights.

Finally, each $g_{\text{cone},j}(\cdot)$ is the smoothed friction cone
constraints for the $j$-th contact point defined by
\begin{equation}
  g_{\text{cone},j}(q, \lambda_j) = \sqrt{\|J_{c,j}^t \lambda_j \|^2 + \epsilon} - \mu_j  J_{c,j}^n \lambda_j
  \label{eq:friction_cone}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon > 0$ is a smoothing parameter, $\mu_j$ is the friction coefficient, and $J_{c,j}^t$ and $J_{c,j}^n$ are the tangential and normal components of the Jacobian of the contact constraint.

\subsection{Upper-Level Problem Optimization on SE(3) Manifold}

Denoting $\lambda^*(q)$ as the optimal $\lambda$ of the lower-level problem for
a given pose $q$, we define the force dependent objective function as
$f_\text{phys}(q) := f_\text{phys}(q, \lambda^*(q))$. Then, using the
\textit{Envelope theorem} \cite{carter2001matheconomics}, we can compute the
gradient of $f_\text{phys}(q)$ with respect to $q$ as follows:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{df_\text{phys}(q)}{dq} = \frac{\partial f_\text{phys}(q, \lambda^*)}{\partial q} + \sum_{j=1}^{n_c} \nu^*_j \frac{\partial g_{\text{cone},j}(q, \lambda^*_j)}{\partial q}
\end{equation*}
where $\nu^*_j$ is the optimal dual variable associated with the $j$-th friction cone constraint $g_{\text{cone},j}(q, \lambda^*_j) \leq 0$.
Using the above gradient, we optimize the placement pose $q$ on SE(3) manifold based on the Riemannian trust-region method \cite{absil2007trust} combined with BFGS Hessian approximation \cite{huang2018riemannianbfgs}.
We use a homotopy schedule to jointly adjust the geometric and physical objective weights, including the force regularization weight $w_\text{reg}$.
Each stage holds these weights fixed and solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:pose_optimization}, initialized from the preceding stage's pose.
The schedule progressively reduces $w_\text{reg}$ to limit regularization-induced equilibrium residuals, and the final stage supplies the pose and contact forces used for candidate evaluation.

\subsection{Lower-Level Problem Optimization with ADMM}

For a fixed $q$, the lower-level problem is a convex optimization problem with
respect to $\lambda$ which can be efficiently solved. Moreover, exploiting the
inherent structure of the lower-level problem, we can reformulate it to be
separable with respect to the objects $\placed_{k+1}$ in the scene and apply
the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
\cite{boyd2011distributed} to solve it in a scalable manner. For each contact
force $\lambda_j$, we introduce two local copies $\lambda_{j,1}$ and
$\lambda_{j,2}$ associated with the two contacting bodies, including the fixed
ground. Specifically, for $j \in I_{l,m}$ with $l < m$, the copies are
associated with bodies $l$ and $m$, respectively. We then impose the consensus
constraints $\lambda_{j,1} = \lambda_{j,2}$. Collecting all local copies into
$\tilde{\lambda}$ makes the force and torque equilibrium term separable by
body: each body's residual depends only on its own local contact-force
variables. The lower-level problem can therefore be reformulated as
\begin{IEEEeqnarray*}{rCl}
  \min_{\tilde{\lambda},z}
  &{\quad}&
  \tilde{f}_\text{phys}(q,\tilde{\lambda})
  + \sum_{\substack{j = 1,\ldots,n_c \\ s = 1,2}}
  \delta_{K_j}(z_{j,s}) \\
  \text{s.t.}
  &{\quad}&
  \lambda_{j,1} = \lambda_{j,2} = z_{j,1} = z_{j,2},
  \qquad j = 1,\ldots,n_c.
\end{IEEEeqnarray*}
where $\tilde{f}_\text{phys}(q,\tilde{\lambda})$ is the extended physical force function and $\delta_{K_j}$ is the indicator function of the convex set of feasible contact forces $K_j := \{\lambda \in \real^3 \mid g_{\text{cone},j}(q,\lambda) \leq 0\}$.
Here, $z_{j,s}$ is an auxiliary variable associated with the $j$-th contact point and $s \in \{1,2\}$.
At each ADMM iteration, the objectwise-separable subproblem decomposes into $|\placed_{k+1}|$ independent subproblems, one for each object $\object_l$.
An equivalent four-dimensional second-order cone (SOC) lifting enables closed-form cone projections for the friction updates, while consensus updates reduce to averaging.

The pose optimization module is implemented using CUDA to leverage GPU parallelization for efficient computation of the gradients and the ADMM updates.
