\section{Pose Optimization} \label{sec:pose_optimization}

The module $\poseopt$ refines $\hat q_{k+1}$ from $\initpose$ toward a stable,
physically feasible placement $q_{k+1}$.

\subsection{Single Object Pose Optimization Problem}

Given $\state_k$, we initialize $q$ at $\hat q_{k+1}$ and solve the bilevel
problem
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
  \min_{q} &{\quad}& \left[f_\mathrm{geo}(q) + \min_{\lambda} f_\mathrm{phys}(q, \lambda)\right] \label{eq:pose_optimization} \\
  \mathrm{s.t. } &{\quad}& g_{\mathrm{cone},j}(q,\lambda_j) \leq 0, \quad j=1,\cdots,n_c \nonumber
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where $q \in \SE(3)$ is the candidate object's pose and $\lambda = (\lambda_1,\cdots,\lambda_{n_c}) \in \real^{3n_c}$ collects all contact forces in the resulting structure, including inter-object and ground contacts.
This formulation follows prior contact-aware optimization methods \cite{lee2025contactfactorgraph,chen2025bodex}.

% The geometric objective $f_\mathrm{geo}(q)$ measures geometric quality, including target shape approximation and collision avoidance; its detailed formulation is provided in the appendix.
The geometric objective $f_\mathrm{geo}$ is designed as follows:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    f_\mathrm{geo}(q)
    ={} & w_\mathrm{h} f_\mathrm{h}(q) + w_\mathrm{in}f_\mathrm{in}(q \mid \target, \object_{i_{k+1}}) \\
        & + w_\mathrm{pen}f_\mathrm{pen}(q \mid \scene_k, \object_{i_{k+1}})
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
with positive weights. For $q=(R,t)$, $f_\mathrm{h}(q)=t_z$ is the world-frame height of the candidate's pose origin.
Writing $\object=\object_{i_{k+1}}$, we penalize proximity to existing objects and the fixed ground as
\begin{equation*}
  f_\mathrm{pen}(q)=\sum_{j\in\placed_k\cup\{i_0\}}
  \bigl[\epsilon_\mathrm{margin}-\sd(\object(q),\object_j(q_j))\bigr]_+^2,
\end{equation*}
where $\sd$ is signed distance (positive for separation), $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$, and $\epsilon_\mathrm{margin}\geq0$ is the clearance margin.
With $\dsf_{\object(q)}(u)=\max_j\dsf_{\convex_{i_{k+1},j}(q)}(u)$, we define
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{aligned}
    m_\mathrm{in}(q) & =\max_j\min_{u\in\mathbb{S}^2}\bigl[\dsf_{\target_j}(u)-\dsf_{\object(q)}(u)\bigr], \\
    f_\mathrm{in}(q) & =\bigl[-m_\mathrm{in}(q)\bigr]_+^2.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
This term penalizes violations of containment of the entire object within a single convex target component, a conservative condition for containment in $\target$.
Noting that the term is differentiable almost everywhere in $q$, we compute pose derivatives at regular inner solutions by implicit differentiation.
Nonsmooth transitions have not caused practical difficulties in our implementation.

Let $\placed_{k+1}:=\placed_k\cup\{i_{k+1}\}$. For distinct $l,m\in\placed_{k+1}\cup\{i_0\}$, let
$I_{l,m}\subset\{1,\ldots,n_c\}$ index contacts between bodies $l$ and $m$.
Each object's contact set
$I_l:=\bigcup_{m\in(\placed_{k+1}\cup\{i_0\})\setminus\{l\}}I_{l,m}$ includes
ground contacts, but the physical objective excludes the ground's equilibrium
residual:
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{l}
  f_\mathrm{phys}(q,\lambda)
  = w_\mathrm{eq}\sum_{l \in \placed_{k+1}} \|J_{c,I_l}^\top(q)  \lambda_{I_l} + \lambda_{\mathrm{ext},l}\|_W^2 \nonumber \\
  \quad + w_\mathrm{cp}\sum_{j \in I_{i_{k+1}}}\sd_j(q)^2 \|\lambda_j\|^2 + w_\mathrm{reg}\sum_{j = 1}^{n_c} \|\lambda_j\|^2
  \label{eq:force_objective}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
The three terms respectively penalize violations of static equilibrium, contact complementarity between the candidate and the current scene, and large contact forces through regularization.
Figure~\ref{fig:pose_optimization_toy} illustrates why equilibrium is evaluated across the structure rather than only for the candidate.
Here, $\sd_j(q)$ is the signed distance between the two bodies associated with contact candidate $j$. $J_{c,I_l}$ is the contact Jacobian for object $\object_l$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{ext},l}$ is its external wrench, $\lambda_{I_l}$ is the vector of contact forces at the contact points in $I_l$, and $w_\mathrm{eq},w_\mathrm{cp},w_\mathrm{reg}>0$ are objective weights.

Finally, each $g_{\mathrm{cone},j}(\cdot)$ is the smoothed friction cone
constraints for the $j$-th contact point defined by
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mathrm{cone},j}(q, \lambda_j) = \sqrt{\|J_{c,j}^t \lambda_j \|^2 + \epsilon} - \mu_j  J_{c,j}^n \lambda_j
  \label{eq:friction_cone}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon > 0$ is a smoothing parameter, $\mu_j$ is the friction coefficient, and $J_{c,j}^t$ and $J_{c,j}^n$ are the tangential and normal components of the Jacobian of the contact constraint.

\subsection{Upper-Level Problem Optimization on SE(3) Manifold}

Let $\lambda^*(q)$ be the solution of the lower-level problem and define $f_\mathrm{phys}(q):=f_\mathrm{phys}(q,\lambda^*(q))$.
The envelope theorem~\cite{carter2001matheconomics} gives
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{df_\mathrm{phys}(q)}{dq} = \frac{\partial f_\mathrm{phys}(q, \lambda^*)}{\partial q} + \sum_{j=1}^{n_c} \nu^*_j \frac{\partial g_{\mathrm{cone},j}(q, \lambda^*_j)}{\partial q}
\end{equation*}
where $\nu^*_j$ is the optimal dual variable for the $j$-th friction cone constraint.
Using the above gradient, we optimize the placement pose $q$ on SE(3) manifold based on the Riemannian trust-region method \cite{absil2007trust} combined with BFGS Hessian approximation \cite{huang2018riemannianbfgs}.
A homotopy schedule adjusts the geometric and physical weights between stages, holding them fixed within each stage and initializing from the preceding pose.
It progressively reduces $w_\mathrm{reg}$ to limit regularization-induced equilibrium residuals. Candidate evaluation uses the final pose and forces.

\subsection{Lower-Level Problem Optimization with ADMM}

For fixed $q$, the lower-level problem is convex in $\lambda$.
To apply bodywise ADMM~\cite{boyd2011distributed}, we introduce two local copies $\lambda_{j,1}$ and $\lambda_{j,2}$ per contact, one for each contacting body, including the ground.
Collecting these copies into $\tilde{\lambda}$ makes each body's equilibrium residual depend only on its local variables.
Consensus and friction constraints give the reformulation
\begin{IEEEeqnarray*}{rCl}
  \min_{\tilde{\lambda},z}
  &{\quad}&
  \tilde{f}_\mathrm{phys}(q,\tilde{\lambda})
  + \sum_{\substack{j = 1,\ldots,n_c \\ s = 1,2}}
  \delta_{K_j}(z_{j,s}) \\
  \mathrm{s.t.}
  &{\quad}&
  \lambda_{j,1} = \lambda_{j,2} = z_{j,1} = z_{j,2},
  \qquad j = 1,\ldots,n_c.
\end{IEEEeqnarray*}
where $\tilde{f}_\mathrm{phys}$ is the physical objective in local variables, $z_{j,s}$ are auxiliary variables, and $\delta_{K_j}$ is the indicator of $K_j := \{\lambda \in \real^3 \mid g_{\mathrm{cone},j}(q,\lambda) \leq 0\}$.
ADMM solves the body subproblems independently.
An equivalent four-dimensional second-order cone (SOC) lifting enables closed-form cone projections for the friction updates, while consensus updates reduce to averaging.

The pose optimization module is implemented using CUDA to leverage GPU parallelization for efficient computation of the gradients and the ADMM updates.
