\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}

Stacking is a fundamental problem with applications such as bin packing, industrial warehouse storage, and stone-wall construction.
In robotic bin packing, container walls can provide lateral support~\cite{wang2022binpacking}.
Without such support, a freestanding structure of $n$ irregular objects must approximate a prescribed target shape $\target\subset\real^3$ while maintaining force and torque equilibrium under gravity through inter-object and ground contacts.
Satisfying these requirements is challenging because pose changes of irregular objects alter both occupied space and contact locations and normals, coupling geometric fit with force and torque equilibrium across the entire structure.
We minimize unfilled target volume subject to containment and physical feasibility:
\begin{gather}
\min_{q_1,\cdots,q_n\in\SE(3)} \vol\left(\target\backslash\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\object_i(q_i)\right)\quad\text{s.t.} \label{eq:problem_definition}\\
c_\text{phys}(q_1,\cdots,q_n)=0,\quad \object_i(q_i)\subseteq\target\ \ \forall i. \nonumber
\end{gather}
where $\object_i(q_i)\subset\real^3$ is the region occupied by object $i$ at pose $q_i\in\SE(3)$, $\vol(\cdot)$ denotes volume, and $c_\text{phys}=0$ represents stability and non-interpenetration constraints.
Constructing such a structure also requires selecting objects, determining which objects support one another, and deciding in what order to place them.
Each placement changes the remaining inventory, available space, and support for subsequent objects, so a currently stable placement may limit the final target coverage.
These interdependent discrete choices make sequence planning combinatorial, while evaluating candidate placements requires iterative numerical evaluation of contact interactions~\cite{furrer2017stacking}, making the search computationally expensive.

For freestanding stone walls, Johns et al.~\cite{johns2020autonomous,johns2023framework} combine masonry heuristics, contact force optimization, geometric alignment, and simulation; Liu and Napp~\cite{liu2023stability} use heuristic filtering and simulation-based structural strength assessments.
These methods assess stability but do not use equilibrium across the entire structure to guide continuous pose refinement or explicitly compare alternative placement sequences.
Menezes et al.~\cite{menezes2021rocks} learn placement values reflecting future rewards, with fixed object sequences and orientations.

We propose a GPU-accelerated stacking framework that integrates structure-wide equilibrium-guided pose refinement with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) over placement sequences (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).
The framework couples continuous placement refinement with discrete lookahead, so candidate poses are optimized before their effects on subsequent placements are evaluated.

For pose refinement, we formulate a differentiable bilevel problem that accounts for how candidate placement affects force and torque equilibrium throughout the structure.
The lower level optimizes shared contact forces across all stones; the upper level refines the candidate pose using gradients of the optimized physical objective and geometric objectives.
Differentiable support functions (DSFs)~\cite{lee2023uncertain,an2024riemannianmtd} provide pose-dependent contact geometry for these gradients.
This formulation builds on contact-force-based manipulation and grasp optimization~\cite{lee2025contactfactorgraph,zurbrugg2025graspqp,chen2025bodex}, extending the optimization to the coupled equilibrium of the growing stack.
In particular, BODex~\cite{chen2025bodex} uses finite differences for coarse contact derivatives and fixed link-frame contact coordinates during fine-stage differentiation; our pose updates differentiate DSF contact geometry.

For sequence planning, we develop a hierarchical MCTS expansion that samples candidate placements, refines their poses, and validates the resulting structures through simulation.
Domain-specific rewards guide lookahead toward target coverage and structural stability, allowing the search to account for how each placement changes future options.
PMBS~\cite{huang2022pmbs} demonstrates batched simulation within MCTS for clutter retrieval; our expansion additionally incorporates structure-wide equilibrium-guided pose optimization before simulation.

To reduce the cost of these repeated evaluations, we parallelize computation both within each structure and across candidates.
We introduce bodywise contact force copies and an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) to decompose the coupled force optimization into parallel bodywise updates, with consensus enforcing contact-force consistency.
Both pose optimization and subADMM simulation~\cite{ji2025gpu} are GPU-accelerated and batched across independent candidates, enabling parallel evaluation of alternative placements throughout the search.
Our main contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}]
\item A differentiable bilevel pose optimization formulation guided by the target shape $T$ and force and torque equilibrium across the entire structure.
\item A stacking-specific MCTS framework that integrates geometry-guided candidate sampling, bilevel pose refinement, and simulation-based evaluation for object selection and placement-sequence planning, with rewards accounting for target volume filling and physical feasibility.
\item GPU-accelerated MCTS evaluation through bodywise ADMM for parallel contact force optimization within structures and batched pose optimization and simulation across candidates.
\end{itemize}
