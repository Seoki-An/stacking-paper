\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}

Stacking is a fundamental problem with applications in bin packing, industrial warehouse storage~\cite{wang2022binpacking,huang2023binpacking}, and stone-wall construction~\cite{johns2020autonomous}.
In freestanding stacking, the resulting structure must approximate a prescribed target shape while maintaining force and torque equilibrium under contact forces and gravity, without support from an enclosing container.
For $n$ irregular objects, this geometric goal can be expressed as minimizing the unfilled volume of a target shape $\target\subset\real^3$, subject to physical feasibility:
\begin{gather}
\min_{q_1,\cdots,q_n\in\SE(3)} \vol\left(\target\backslash\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\object_i(q_i)\right)\quad\text{s.t.} \label{eq:problem_definition}\\
c_\text{phys}(\object_1(q_1),\cdots,\object_n(q_n))=0, \nonumber
\end{gather}
where $\object_i(q_i)\subset\real^3$ is the region occupied by object $i$ at pose $q_i\in\SE(3)$, $\vol(\cdot)$ denotes volume, and $c_\text{phys}=0$ represents stability and non-interpenetration constraints.
Achieving these requirements simultaneously is challenging because changing one object pose alters both the occupied space and the contact geometry through which loads are transmitted across the structure.
Moreover, the choice and pose of lower objects constrain the remaining feasible subsequent placements, so even a currently stable arrangement may limit further stacking toward the target shape, especially when the available object inventory is limited.
Planning its construction therefore involves combinatorial choices of objects and placement order, together with iterative numerical evaluation of contact interactions~\cite{furrer2017stacking}, making planning computationally expensive.

Robotic bin-packing methods consider placement stability within containers~\cite{wang2022binpacking,huang2023binpacking}, where enclosing walls can provide lateral support unavailable in freestanding construction.
More directly related, Johns et al.~\cite{johns2020autonomous,johns2023framework} construct freestanding stone walls using contact-aware registration and simulation, while Liu and Napp~\cite{liu2023stability} combine heuristic filtering with simulation-based assessments of structural strength to select placements.
These stone-stacking methods do not use equilibrium across the entire structure to guide continuous pose refinement.
Furthermore, they select placements using the current structure without explicitly comparing subsequent placement sequences, although masonry-inspired rules encourage suitable support for later objects.
This leaves two challenges: refining poses according to contact-force redistribution throughout the structure and evaluating how current choices affect future feasible placements.

We address these challenges through pose optimization incorporating structural stability and GPU-accelerated Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for sequence planning.
Unlike prior stacking methods, we formulate placement refinement as a constrained bilevel problem and compute pose gradients of the objective and constraints using differentiable support functions (DSFs) for shape representation and collision detection~\cite{lee2023uncertain,an2024riemannianmtd}.
Bilevel formulations also appear in contact-aware manipulation planning and grasp generation~\cite{lee2025contactfactorgraph,zurbrugg2025graspqp,chen2025bodex}.
Our lower-level problem accounts for force and torque equilibrium of all stones, including the candidate, with shared contact forces transmitting loads through the stack to the ground.

Evaluating how current placements affect subsequent stacking through MCTS requires repeated pose refinement and structural simulation, making lookahead computationally demanding.

To make lookahead search computationally practical for freestanding stacking, we introduce bodywise contact-force copies and an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) decomposition for parallel force optimization within each candidate structure, while batching independent candidate placements on the GPU.
Together with GPU-accelerated subADMM simulation~\cite{ji2025gpu}, this enables parallel candidate evaluation throughout the search tree (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
Heuristic leaf evaluation replaces costly rollouts, while task-specific rewards guide the search toward target coverage and structural stability.

Our main contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate a single pose refinement for freestanding stacking as a constrained bilevel placement problem, using differentiable contact features and geometric derivatives to guide pose refinement with target geometry and whole-structure equilibrium residuals.
\item We introduce a bodywise ADMM decomposition of the contact-force problem, enabling GPU-parallel solves within each candidate structure and across candidate placements.
\item We develop a GPU-accelerated MCTS planner that significantly accelerates lookahead search over object choices and placement poses through parallel pose optimization and structural simulation.
\end{itemize}
