\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}

Automated packing and palletizing have long been studied to improve space utilization and material handling in logistics and manufacturing~\cite{dowsland1990palletloading}.
Recent robotic packing methods accommodate irregular objects while considering packing density, stability, and placement feasibility~\cite{wang2022binpacking,huang2023binpacking}.
Constructing freestanding structures from natural stones requires finding mutually supporting arrangements among uneven surfaces while achieving a prescribed target shape without support from container walls.

Several stone-stacking methods use placement criteria developed for particular construction settings, including tower-building objectives~\cite{furrer2017stacking} and masonry-inspired geometric rules~\cite{johns2020autonomous,liu2021drystacking}.
For example, Johns et al.'s double-faced wall planner generates candidates along the intersection of the target wall surfaces and the existing structure, and applies inward-slope heuristics tailored to wall construction~\cite{johns2020autonomous}.
The ability to construct freeform walls~\cite{johns2023framework} demonstrates flexibility in wall geometry, while extending beyond such construction settings motivates planning driven by target geometry without wall-specific placement rules.
Structural stability also requires reasoning beyond the support of the newly placed object: its load can affect the equilibrium of objects beneath it.
Liu and Napp~\cite{liu2023stability} explicitly address structural strength during planning, demonstrating the value of evaluating the structure as a whole.
However, assessing the current structure alone does not determine how a placement affects future support and space utilization.
Menezes et al.~\cite{menezes2021rocks} address sequential placement through learned action values that account for future rewards, with a prescribed object sequence and fixed orientations.
We investigate explicit lookahead over object choices and placement poses, coupled with structural equilibrium evaluation and target-shape objectives.

We consider stacking a finite inventory of $n$ irregular objects to approximate a target region $\target\subset\real^3$.
Let $\object_i(q_i)\subset\real^3$ denote the volume occupied by object $i$ at pose $q_i\in\SE(3)$.
The final configuration objective is
\begin{gather}
\min_{q_1,\cdots,q_n\in\SE(3)} \vol\left(\target\backslash\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\object_i(q_i)\right)\quad\text{s.t.} \label{eq:problem_definition}\\
c_\text{phys}(\object_1(q_1),\cdots,\object_n(q_n))=0, \nonumber
\end{gather}
where $\vol(\cdot)$ denotes volume and $c_\text{phys}=0$ represents stability and non-interpenetration constraints.
Jointly optimizing all object poses under coupled physical constraints is computationally demanding.
To obtain a practical planning procedure, we decompose construction into sequential decisions, selecting one object and optimizing its placement at each step while checking the resulting structure's physical feasibility.
This decomposition reduces the dimension of each pose optimization, but each placement changes the support geometry, free space, and remaining inventory.
Multi-step lookahead allows the planner to evaluate these future consequences beyond immediate placement quality.
Our central question is how to combine this lookahead with structural equilibrium evaluation in a common framework for different target shapes.

We address this question with a framework coupling bilevel pose optimization and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS).
The pose optimizer refines geometric proposals using target-shape objectives and a physical objective that evaluates force and torque equilibrium across the structure under friction constraints.
The target enters through its geometric representation, allowing the same optimization formulation to be applied to different target shapes.
A bodywise ADMM decomposition enables parallel solution of the contact-force subproblem.
The sequence planner embeds pose refinement and physical simulation in batched, receding-horizon MCTS, following prior work on batched simulation-based search~\cite{huang2022pmbs}.
Simulation supplies settled successor states and rejects unstable placements, while multi-step lookahead compares their effects on subsequent construction.

We hypothesize that combining structural equilibrium evaluation with multi-step lookahead improves target coverage while maintaining stability across different target geometries under a fixed planning budget.
Comparisons with greedy placement and candidate-only equilibrium evaluation will test the effects of lookahead and structural coupling, respectively.
Evaluations across target shapes and search horizons will examine the applicability of the common formulation and its computational tradeoffs.
The proposed contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A bilevel placement optimizer that combines explicit target geometry with contact-force optimization across the structure, using bodywise ADMM updates for GPU computation.
  \item A batched, receding-horizon stacking planner that integrates pose refinement and physical simulation with multi-step lookahead over object choices and placement poses.
\end{itemize}
